An article becomes something you can listen to.

Think of Article Reader as a reading desk and a separate narrator. The desk finds the words, the narrator speaks them, and your browser plays the recording.

From link to listening

Choose an English or French voice that matches the text. Changing voices does not translate the article. When a page hides its content behind scripts or a paywall, pasting text is the fallback.

You do not have to wait for the whole article

If the narrator is slower than your listening speed, the player may need to buffer. Faster playback does not make the speech model work faster. These pictures show the idea, not measured performance.

Three controls, three different jobs

Pause / Resume

Like putting a finger on your place in a book. Listening pauses; the narrator can keep preparing the recording.

Stop

Throw away this recording and cancel future parts. Speech already being made on the separate service may still finish.

Read again

Make a fresh recording from the article text already on the desk. After Stop, you can edit that text first; the link is not fetched again.

Reload recovery is a bookmark, not a downloaded book

Your browser saves a reference to the recording and its best-known listening position. The article and audio stay temporarily on the reader server, not in that browser bookmark.

On reload, the reader checks whether the same recording still exists and offers it paused near that position. Tap Resume to continue without asking the narrator to start over. A sleeping phone may not save every moment, so the position can lag.

The bookmark cannot bring back an expired or stopped recording, or one lost when the reader server restarts. This is not offline listening. Use one playback tab.

Where the words go

The reader server fetches the article. The separate speech service receives the words it must speak. That service can run on your machine or on a hosted endpoint; provider credentials stay on the reader server.

Keep the reader behind a VPN or another access-control layer. It does not have built-in user authentication or per-user rate limits.
